      How to interpret the analysis

      Each area uses a different measure: calendars use recorded hours; Things uses completed-task counts with your configured filters. Operations is split into Sleep & rest and Daily life rather than counted twice. Growth includes workouts only. Summary is an aggregate, so it is not treated as another independent area.

      Only complete UTC dates before the oldest available fetch date are included. Each pair starts at the later of its two first recorded dates. Within that span, a missing entry is treated as zero recorded activity; the app cannot distinguish an unlogged day from a true zero. Empty or unavailable areas are not evidence of inactivity. Calendar sessions are assigned to their UTC start date, including overnight rest; they are not split across midnight. Overlapping entries can exceed 24 hours.

      Raw Spearman correlation uses average ranks for ties. The adjusted measure correlates those ranks after removing each weekday's mean rank within each series. This reduces weekly-routine effects but does not control for deadlines, illness, trends, or other common causes. Next-day comparisons align A on date t with B on t+1; the arrow describes timing, not causation.

      The exploratory screen requires 28 paired dates, at least 7 active dates in each area, absolute adjusted correlation ≥0.30, the same direction with magnitude ≥0.15 in both halves, and adjusted p ≤0.10. Approximate p-values compare all circular shifts of weekday-adjusted series, preserving serial order. This assumes roughly stationary series and is unreliable under changing routines or long-term trends. Benjamini–Yekutieli correction is applied across all eligible same-day and both-direction next-day comparisons in the selected window. Short histories give coarse p-values and may yield no screened patterns. Switching windows and repeated visits are additional exploration, not corrected confirmatory tests.

      “Higher days” means the first area exceeds its median, compared with days at or below it. Differences are descriptive, unadjusted, and shown in the second area's units. Half-period consistency is a sensitivity check, not independent replication. Use these results to formulate a small experiment; do not infer that more of an activity will cause an improvement.

      Method references: Spearman correlation · Multiple-comparison correction.
